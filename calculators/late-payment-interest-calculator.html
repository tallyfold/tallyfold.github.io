What you are allowed to charge

GOV.UK says: “The interest you can charge if another business is late paying for goods or a service is ‘statutory interest’ - this is 8% plus the Bank of England base rate for business to business transactions.” It also says you cannot claim statutory interest if a different rate of interest is set in a contract, and that you cannot use a lower rate in a contract with a public authority.

On top of the interest you can charge a fixed sum for the cost of recovering the payment. GOV.UK sets it by the size of the debt, and says you can only charge it once for each payment:

Amount of debtWhat you can charge
Up to £999.99£40
£1,000 to £9,999.99£70
£10,000 or more£100

GOV.UK adds that a supplier “can also claim for reasonable costs each time you try to recover the debt”, on top of that fixed sum. It does not put a figure on those, so this calculator leaves them out.

When a payment counts as late

GOV.UK says that if you agree a payment date it “must usually be within 30 days for public authorities or 60 days for business transactions”, and that a longer period than 60 days can be agreed between businesses but “must be fair to both businesses”. Where no payment date is agreed, it says the payment is late 30 days after either the customer gets the invoice, or you deliver the goods or provide the service, whichever is later.

How the interest is worked out

  1. Add 8% to the Bank of England base rate. With a base rate of 4%, that is 12% a year.
  2. Take that percentage of the unpaid amount. 12% of £2,400 is £288 for a full year.
  3. Divide by 365 for the daily amount. £288 divided by 365 is 78.9p a day.
  4. Multiply by the days the payment is late. Thirty days at 78.9p is £23.67.
  5. Add the fixed debt recovery sum for the size of the debt. £2,400 falls in the £1,000 to £9,999.99 band, so £70, making £93.67 in all.

The fixed sum is far larger than a month of interest on an ordinary invoice, which is worth knowing before you spend an afternoon on the arithmetic.

Before you charge it

  • These rules are for business-to-business debts. They are not for money owed by a private customer.
  • Check the contract or your own terms first. GOV.UK says you cannot claim statutory interest where a contract sets a different rate of interest.
  • Charging interest is your decision, not an obligation. Many small firms send one more polite reminder first and keep the right to charge in reserve.
  • Whatever you decide, write it down: the invoice date, the date it was due, every reminder you sent and what was said.
