What yield means

Yield is the yearly rent set against what the property is worth, written as a percentage. It is the figure that lets you compare one property with another, or with leaving the money somewhere else. On its own it says nothing about whether a property is a good buy: it ignores what happens to the price, and it ignores your own tax.

Gross yield is the yearly rent divided by the value. It is the headline figure in most adverts, and it flatters, because no property earns rent with nothing going out.

Net yield takes the running costs off the rent first, and allows for the weeks the property sits empty between tenants. It is the more honest number, and it is usually one to two percentage points lower.

How it is worked out

  1. Yearly rent is the monthly rent times twelve. £875 a month is £10,500.
  2. Empty weeks come off that. Two weeks at £875 a month is about £404, leaving £10,096.
  3. Running costs come off too. £165 a month is £1,980 a year, leaving £8,116.
  4. Gross yield is the full yearly rent divided by the value: £10,500 divided by £185,000 is 5.7%.
  5. Net yield is what is left divided by the value: £8,116 divided by £185,000 is 4.4%.

What is not in these figures

  • Mortgage payments. Neither yield includes them. Yield measures the property, not how you paid for it.
  • Tax. The figures here are before any tax of any kind. What you owe depends on your own circumstances: see GOV.UK or an accountant.
  • What the property does in price. A low yield in an area where prices rise can beat a high yield where they do not. Yield says nothing either way.
  • The big occasional costs. A boiler, a roof or a void of several months will not show up in a monthly running-cost figure. Set money aside for them and put that figure in the running costs.

Keeping the real figures

A yield is a forecast. What it actually did is a different question, and it is the one the tax return asks. The Landlord Rental Income and Expense Tracker logs the rent and the costs for each property over a tax year and adds them up under the box numbers printed on HMRC's UK property pages.
