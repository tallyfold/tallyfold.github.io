Day rate calculator

Work out the day rate and hourly rate to charge when you work for yourself. Start from the income you want, add what the business costs to run, and divide by the days you can actually bill.

Day rate to charge, before VAT

£285.71

How it is worked out

  1. What the business has to bring in is the income you want plus its costs. £40,000 plus £6,000 is £46,000.
  2. Working days are the days you work each week times the weeks you work. 5 days for 46 weeks is 230 days.
  3. Billable days are the share of those you can charge for. 70% of 230 is 161 days.
  4. Day rate is the first figure divided by the billable days. £46,000 divided by 161 is £285.71.
  5. Hourly rate is the day rate divided by the hours in a day. £285.71 divided by 7.5 is £38.10.

Why you cannot bill every day

An employee is paid for every working day. When you work for yourself, only the days a customer pays for bring money in. Quoting, travelling, chasing invoices, doing the books, buying materials and waiting for the next job all take days that nobody pays for. The fewer days you can bill, the more each one has to earn.

What to put in

BoxInclude
Income you wantWhat you want the business to pay you in a year, before Income Tax and National Insurance. If you want to match a salary, remember a salary usually comes with paid holiday, sick pay and an employer's pension payment, and this figure does not.
Business costsEverything the business pays for in a year that you do not charge to a customer separately: vehicle, insurance, tools, phone, software, accountant, training, and anything you want to put by for a pension or quiet months.
Weeks not workedHoliday, bank holidays and an allowance for illness. Nobody pays you for these.
Share you can billLook back at the last few months and count the days you charged for. If you are starting out, be cautious.

What it leaves out

The calculator does not work out tax. The income figure is before Income Tax and National Insurance, and the rates are before VAT. It gives the rate you need to reach your figures. It does not tell you what customers in your trade and area will pay, so compare the answer with what others charge.
