Markup and margin are not the same

Both describe the same profit, measured against different things. Markup is profit as a percentage of your cost. Margin is profit as a percentage of your price. A job that costs £100 and sells for £150 has a 50% markup and a 33.3% margin.

Mixing them up is a common way to undercharge. Adding 30% to your costs does not give a 30% margin. It gives 23.1%.

How it is worked out

  1. Total cost is direct cost plus overhead. £1,000 with 15% overhead is £1,150.
  2. Price is total cost divided by (1 minus the margin). £1,150 divided by 0.70 is £1,642.86.
  3. Profit is price minus total cost: £492.86, which is 30% of the price.

Overhead is the running cost you carry on every job, such as a van, insurance, tools and phone. Set it to 0 if your cost figure already includes it.
