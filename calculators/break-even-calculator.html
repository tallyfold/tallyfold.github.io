What break-even means

Break-even is the point where the money coming in exactly covers the money going out. Below it you are paying to stay open. Above it, every extra sale adds to profit.

Each sale leaves something behind after its own costs. A print that sells for £45 and costs £18 to make and send leaves £27. That £27 is called the contribution: it goes towards the fixed costs first, and only after those are covered does it become profit.

How it is worked out

  1. Contribution from one sale is the price minus the cost of that sale. £45 minus £18 is £27.
  2. Break-even sales are the fixed costs divided by the contribution, rounded up to a whole sale. £1,800 divided by £27 is 66.7, so 67 sales.
  3. Break-even takings are those sales multiplied by the price: 67 at £45 is £3,015 a month.
  4. For a profit, add the profit you want to the fixed costs before dividing. For £1,000 a month: £2,800 divided by £27 is 103.7, so 104 sales.

Getting the figures right

  • Count your own pay as a fixed cost. A business that only breaks even without paying you is not breaking even.
  • If you sell many different things, use an average sale: a typical order value and the typical cost of filling it.
  • If you sell time, one sale is one hour or one day, and its cost is whatever that hour costs you in travel, materials or fees.
  • Put yearly bills in as a monthly share. An insurance bill of £360 a year is £30 a month.
